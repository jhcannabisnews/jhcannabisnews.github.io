Cannabis is on your ballot: every vote that matters on November 3

Houston votes on marijuana arrests, Idaho votes on whether citizens can ever legalize it themselves, and Massachusetts decides whether to end legal sales. Plus Ohio's new limits.

Houston: Proposition A

Houston voters will decide whether low-level marijuana arrests should be the police department's lowest priority, written into the city charter.

Proposition A would not make marijuana legal. Possession is still a crime under Texas law, and officers can still arrest you. It changes police priorities.

Supporters, led by Ground Game Texas, gathered about 40,000 signatures and say low-level arrests have risen under the current city administration. The Houston police officers' union calls it unnecessary, since prosecutors already send most of these cases to diversion programs.

The twist: in 2025 a Texas appeals court sided with the state against local measures like this one, and cities such as Dallas and Denton have declined to enforce theirs. Even a yes vote may start a legal fight over whether Houston can actually do it. Early voting in Texas starts October 19.

Idaho: HJR 4

Idaho is one of the last states where marijuana is completely illegal. A yes vote on HJR 4 amends the state constitution so that only the legislature can legalize marijuana or any other drug; citizens would permanently lose the ability to do it by ballot initiative. A no vote keeps the initiative process as it is.

Lawmakers passed it 58–10 in the House and 29–6 in the Senate, and a medical marijuana initiative fell short of the ballot this summer. Supporters say it keeps Idaho from becoming "the next Portland or Denver." Opponents say voters shouldn't sign away their own rights. Either way, it's a vote on who gets to decide.

Massachusetts: Question 8 update

Question 8 would end recreational marijuana sales. U.S. Senators Elizabeth Warren and Ed Markey say they're voting no, joining Governor Maura Healey. Meanwhile, ten years after legalization, state regulators are moving toward rules for cannabis consumption lounges, the first in New England. Our full Question 8 breakdown →

Ohio: new limits

New limits on recreational marijuana took effect in Ohio, including a 90% THC cap on concentrates and a one-ounce limit per package of flower. Some high-potency vapes are off shelves entirely; one shop owner said they had to destroy hundreds of dollars of product. Customers say the same amount can now cost more, and shop owners say they're struggling to keep up with how often the rules change.

Around the states

  • Michigan: regulators revoked a dispensary's license and fined it $251,000 after employees allegedly gave 887 vape cartridges to two social media influencers.
  • New York: recreational sales have passed $4.1 billion since late 2022.
  • Pennsylvania: the Republican Senate leader says the state can't legalize without national direction.
  • Wisconsin: a Democratic candidate for governor says legalization could help fund pre-K and health care.
  • Florida: two new legalization amendments were filed, aiming for 2028.

Federal update

The government's response in the paused DEA rescheduling hearing was due October 13; it decides how long the delay could last. Separately, the challenge to April's move of medical marijuana to Schedule III is in a federal appeals court, where the Justice Department and opponents agreed on a briefing schedule. Representative Brian Mast, a Florida Republican who co-chairs the Cannabis Caucus, wants the review expanded to psychedelics. And the hemp clock keeps ticking: November 12 for cannabinoids the plant can't make (like THC-O), and December 11 for gummies, drinks, THCA flower and most likely delta-8.

What it means for you

  • Houston: early voting starts October 19. A yes on Proposition A changes police priorities; it doesn't legalize.
  • Idaho: the question isn't whether to legalize, it's who decides in the future.
  • Ohio: check package limits before you shop.

Update (Oct 13, 2026): the episode says delta-8 loses federal hemp status on November 12.
