EPISODE 30 · NOVEMBER 12, 2026

Is your CBD oil illegal after Dec 11? The 0.4 mg rule, explained

On December 11, 2026, the federal definition of hemp caps a finished product at 0.4 milligrams of THC per container. Per bottle, not per drop. Here is what likely fails, what likely survives and what is still unclear, product by product, plus where Congress, the FDA and Texas shops stand.

Up front: this is news, not legal advice. "Likely" is our read of the reporting, not a ruling, and the law doesn't name CBD. Nothing here says a product you own is illegal; we haven't found a clear federal rule on what you already own, and we won't guess.

1. The rule in plain words

Last November's federal funding law rewrote the definition of hemp. A finished hemp-derived product with more than 0.4 milligrams "combined total per container" of THC and cannabinoids with similar effects falls outside that definition, as the Congressional Research Service quotes the statute. Outside hemp means federal law would treat the product as marijuana.

A container is the innermost packaging in direct contact with the product for retail sale, so a bottle would count as one container. THCA counts toward total THC. Congress delayed most of the change from Nov 12 to Dec 11 in a bill signed Sept 2, and Dec 11 is 29 days after today. For the full background, see our federal hemp ban explainer.

2. Bottle by bottle: what likely fails, what likely survives

  • Full-spectrum CBD oil: likely fails. Full-spectrum means the product keeps the plant's other compounds, and those products typically contain small amounts of THC. Healthline reported that the limits would sweep up many CBD products, especially full-spectrum ones, and that they could be banned. The Hemp Law Group calls CBD "a grayer zone" that depends on the formula and on how the container limit is applied. So we say likely, not certain.
  • Zero-THC isolate: likely survives. A product with no THC at all, such as an isolate or a broad-spectrum product that truly tests at zero, would be under the cap. The FDA's list of similar cannabinoids is still missing, so even that isn't certain.
  • "THC-free" label: the lab report decides. See the next section.
  • Topicals and creams: unclear. KPRC lists some topicals as possibly affected, and the Stafford shop owner quoted below still sells her creams.
  • A jar of 20 gummies at 5 mg each: 250 times the cap. One 5 mg gummy alone is 12.5 times 0.4 mg. The multiples are the Hemp Law Group's math, and they treat the jar as one container.
  • A 10 mg hemp THC drink: 25 times the cap.
  • THCA flower: fails. The new test counts THCA as THC, and a Texas CBD shop co-founder told the San Antonio Current that raw flower will fail it starting Dec 11.

"Over the cap" here is arithmetic against the Dec 11 limit, not a legal ruling on any product.

3. Why a "THC-free" label doesn't settle it

Daniel Kruger, a University at Buffalo researcher, told Healthline that some products that may have a minimal amount of THC turn out to have a lot more. A 2023 study found CBD products frequently contained small amounts of THC even when labeled as having none. That is why the lab report tells you more than the label. If you shop for hemp, ask for it, and look for total THC for the whole container, not per serving.

4. What changes today, Nov 12

One piece of the new definition was not delayed. Starting Nov 12, products with cannabinoids the plant can't naturally make lose federal hemp status. That is separate from the 0.4 mg cap. Sources disagree on whether delta-8 and HHC are covered by it. In Texas, delta-8 and similar isomers have been controlled substances since July 31, 2026, either way.

5. The missing FDA list

The FDA was supposed to publish a list of the cannabinoids it covers and more detail on what counts as a container. The law gave it 90 days, which made the deadline Feb 10. As of Nov 9, the Hemp Law Group said it still hadn't been published, nearly nine months late. Until it is, what counts as one container could still shift.

6. Congress: one plan, five bills, no movement

Rep. Morgan Griffith of Virginia told Roll Call he wants his hemp regulation bill inside a budget reconciliation package before Dec 11. Roll Call reported it could be paired with Rep. Beth Van Duyne of Texas's bill to regulate and tax hemp THC drinks.

As of Nov 9, five hemp bills sat in committee and none had advanced, per the Hemp Law Group: one would delay the change two years, one would repeal it, one would regulate instead, one covers hemp drinks, and one is a duty on Chinese hemp products. Griffith said Rep. Andy Harris and Sen. Ted Budd, among others, want to stop the delay. Sen. Cynthia Lummis told Roll Call that syncing the House and Senate on a package would be "very challenging."

There are pressures on both sides. President Trump asked Congress in April to update the law so Americans can keep access to full-spectrum CBD products. In June, budget director Russ Vought wrote to Speaker Johnson asking for fair treatment of hemp products, Marijuana Moment reported. On the other side, Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker, his lieutenant governor and Sen. Tammy Duckworth urged Congress to let the change take effect with no further delay. KPRC summarized the restriction side this way: some intoxicating hemp products were sold with weak oversight, raising concerns about safety, labeling and access by minors.

7. Texas shops: estimates, not counts

Sandra Campbell of CBD American Shaman in Stafford told KPRC she has pulled an estimated 20 to 40 of about 50 products and probably can't stay in business. Her CBD oils and creams are still on the shelf. Carlton Bynum, a veteran and former shop owner who is in a group of about 300 to 400 Texas owners, estimates 20 to 30% have already closed. "If the December 11th ban hits," he told KPRC, he could see 60 to 70% closing. KPRC notes that is his estimate, not a verified statewide projection.

Mark Bordas of the Texas Hemp Business Council told KSAT he is about seven in ten confident the full range comes back, an industry view.

8. Texas rules in court, and what Texas leaders can't change

The health department's hemp rules took effect March 31. They raised the manufacturer fee from $258 to $10,000 per facility and retail registration from $155 to $5,000, the Texas Tribune reported. They're in court now, and the Fifteenth Court of Appeals canceled its Dec 8 argument, to be reset "at a later date."

Texas leaders can't touch a federal date. Vikki Goodwin beat Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick by six points, KSAT reported, and her listed priorities include legalizing cannabis, hemp and THC, with age limits, labeling and testing. The session opens Jan 12, 2027. One CBD shop co-founder told the San Antonio Current: "Don't assume the election fixed this."

9. Zero hemp ban bills filed, as of Wednesday

As of Wednesday, Nov 11, we found no hemp or THC ban bill among the filings for the new Texas session. We checked the filed-bill lists by caption, so treat it as a snapshot. Filing runs to March 12, 2027, so that can change any day. More on the Texas filings.

Quick answers

Is CBD oil illegal after Dec 11, 2026?

The law doesn't name CBD, but it can reach CBD products that contain THC. Full-spectrum oil likely fails; that is our read, not a ruling.

Will zero-THC or isolate CBD products survive?

Likely, but the FDA's list of similar cannabinoids isn't published, so it isn't certain.

Does a "THC-free" label mean a product passes?

No. The lab report, with total THC for the whole container, is the better guide.

What changes on Nov 12, 2026?

Products with cannabinoids the plant can't naturally make lose federal hemp status. The cap starts Dec 11.

Has a Texas hemp ban bill been filed for 2027?

Not that we found, as of Wednesday, Nov 11. Bills can be filed until March 12, 2027.

If a hemp ban bill is filed, or Congress votes, we'll say so.
