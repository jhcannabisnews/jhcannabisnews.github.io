Delta-8: how it became legal and why it's going away

For five years delta-8 was sold in gas stations in states where marijuana is a crime. Here's how that happened, and how 2026 ends it.

What delta-8 is

Delta-8 THC is a close chemical cousin of delta-9, the main high-causing compound in marijuana. It's intoxicating, though users generally describe it as milder. The plant only makes trace amounts, so nearly all commercial delta-8 is made in a lab by chemically converting CBD extracted from legal hemp.

Why it was sold legally

The 2018 Farm Bill legalized hemp and its "derivatives, extracts, cannabinoids, isomers" as long as delta-9 THC stayed at or under 0.3%. It said nothing about delta-8. Sellers argued that delta-8 made from hemp was hemp, and in 2022 a federal appeals court (the Ninth Circuit, in AK Futures v. Boyd Street Distro) agreed that the plain text of the law covered it. The DEA has long said synthetically derived THC stays controlled, which kept delta-8 in a gray zone, but the products stayed on shelves.

The pushback

  • Safety: the FDA has warned about delta-8 products. They're unregulated, often sold in candy-style packaging, and the conversion process can leave byproducts.
  • States: many banned or restricted it on their own over the years.
  • Texas, July 31, 2026: delta-8 and delta-10 went on the state's Schedule I. Cannabis attorneys say possession can be charged as a state jail felony (180 days to 2 years).

The federal end date

Most likely Dec 11, 2026. Under §781 of the fiscal 2026 federal spending law, cannabinoids made outside the plant are no longer hemp, in all 50 states. The September 2026 stopgap kept Nov 12 only for cannabinoids the plant can't make naturally (like THC-O) and moved the rest to Dec 11. Because delta-8 occurs naturally in trace amounts, lawyers read delta-8 made from CBD as a Dec 11 product, but that depends on an FDA list of natural cannabinoids that still isn't out. Some trackers still say Nov 12, so don't count on the extra month.
