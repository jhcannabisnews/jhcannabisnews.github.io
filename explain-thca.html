THCA vs. delta-9: why "hemp flower" gets you high

THCA flower and dispensary weed can be chemically the same thing once lit. The difference was a lab test, and that test is about to change.

What THCA is

Living cannabis makes very little delta-9 THC directly. It makes THCA (tetrahydrocannabinolic acid), the raw acid form. THCA on its own isn't intoxicating.

Heat turns it into THC

When THCA is heated by smoking, vaping or baking, it loses part of its molecule (a process called decarboxylation) and becomes delta-9 THC. That's why raw flower can test low in delta-9 and still produce a full high when smoked.

The math labs use

Total THC = delta-9 THC + (THCA × 0.877)

The 0.877 accounts for the weight lost when THCA converts. Example: flower testing 0.2% delta-9 and 20% THCA works out to about 17.7% total THC, the same range as dispensary marijuana.

How it got sold as hemp

The hemp definition's 0.3% limit was written around delta-9 THC. Federal crop rules already measured total THC at harvest, but much of the retail market relied on product tests that counted delta-9 alone. That gap is how high-THCA flower landed in smoke shops across the country as "hemp."

What changes

  • Federal, Dec 11, 2026 (as industry trackers report): hemp is defined by total THC, so THCA counts. Most THCA flower stops qualifying as hemp anywhere.
  • Texas, July 31, 2026: THCA flower is on the state's Schedule I. Cannabis attorneys say possession can be charged as a state jail felony.
  • Other states already test total THC or ban smokable hemp, and the list keeps shifting. Check the state map and your state's agriculture or health department.
