How to fight for legal cannabis in Texas, step by step

Texas has no citizen petitions, so the people who can change cannabis law are your own state lawmakers. Here is the five-minute way to reach them, plus answers to the questions viewers sent this week.

Why your call matters here. Texas has no citizen petitions. Only the Legislature can put a constitutional amendment on the ballot, and that takes two thirds of both chambers. The session opens January 12, 2027.

Step by step: reach your two lawmakers

  1. Find your Texas House member and state senator with the Legislature's own lookup, Who Represents Me. You type your address there, not on our site.
  2. Call. Our Contact Lawmakers page has a 30-second script: fill in your name, your city and your lawmaker's name, then add your own reason.
  3. Email. Same idea: copy it, fill in the blanks, say why it matters to you, and send it. Call, email or both.

Be kind. A staffer usually answers the phone and is just doing the job. Polite and clear works best.

Why there is no ballot route

Texas has no citizen initiative, so voters cannot put legalization on the ballot themselves. A statewide vote needs a constitutional amendment, which needs two thirds of all members elected to each house (Art. XVII, Sec. 1). Three legalization bills are already filed for the 90th Legislature: HB 362, HB 369 and SB 237. See our bill explainer.

What Dec 11 means for states where cannabis is already legal

Licensed dispensaries in those states operate under state law, and the federal hemp change does not touch them. It hits hemp-derived THC sold outside that system. From Dec 11, a finished hemp product is capped at 0.4 mg of total THC per container, and THCA counts toward the total (P.L. 119-37, Sec. 781). Delta-8 made from CBD is out entirely. Oregon legalized adult use by ballot measure in 2014, a route Texas does not have.

Can the ban be beaten in court?

A straight constitutional challenge is an uphill fight: in Gonzales v. Raich (2005) the Supreme Court held that Congress can reach even home-grown marijuana for a patient's own use. Texas hemp businesses did sue the state health department in April 2026 over its THCA rules. At our last check, those rules were in effect while the state's appeal continued.

How big is the Texas medical program?

DPS reports 180,299 patients listed since 2017, a lifetime count, and 989 registered physicians. That is under 1 percent of Texas's roughly 31.7 million people. Doses are capped at 10 mg of THC. The 2025 law added conditions, including chronic pain, but patients still need a registered doctor and a prescription, and there is no home grow. Calling the program too small is James's opinion; the numbers are here for you to judge.

THCA, plants and the HB 362 timeline

THCA is the acid form of THC as it occurs in the raw plant; heat turns it into THC. Texas health rules have counted it since March 31, and the federal cap counts it from Dec 11. On home growing, HB 362 would allow up to 12 plants per residence; in our community poll (99 votes) 59 percent picked 12 per home, 35 percent 12 per adult, 4 percent 6, and 2 percent no home grow.

If HB 362 passed as written, it would take effect September 1, 2027, with state rules due July 1, 2028 and licensing by November 1, 2028. Its 20 percent retail tax would split 25 percent to veterans' assistance, 15 percent to addiction prevention and treatment, 10 percent to cities and counties with a cannabis business, and the rest to the school fund. Property tax relief is not in the bill.

Frequently asked questions

How do I contact my Texas lawmakers about cannabis?

Use the Texas Legislature's Who Represents Me lookup to find your House member and senator, then call or email using the script on our Contact Lawmakers page.

Can Texas voters put cannabis legalization on the ballot?

No. Texas has no citizen initiative. Only the Legislature can send a constitutional amendment to voters, with two thirds of both chambers.

Does the Dec 11 hemp change affect licensed dispensaries in other states?

No. Licensed dispensaries operate under state law. The federal change targets hemp-derived THC products sold outside that system.

When could HB 362 take effect?

If passed as written, September 1, 2027, with licensing required to begin by November 1, 2028. It is a filed bill, not law.
