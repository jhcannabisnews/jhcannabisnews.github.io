EPISODE 36 · WED NOV 18, 2026

The hemp ban already started: what got banned Nov 12, and what didn't

Videos claim THCA and delta-8 are banned right now. Under most legal readings, not yet. What did change on Nov 12 is narrower: cannabinoids a cannabis plant can't make on its own lost federal hemp status.

Short answer: THCA flower, most delta-8, delta-9 gummies and THC drinks stay on the legal side of the federal line until December 11. What left on November 12 is THC-O and other cannabinoids the plant cannot make on its own.

What actually changed on Nov 12

Last year's hemp law rewrote the definition of hemp. In September the stopgap funding bill pushed almost all of it back to Dec 11, but one piece stayed: a cannabinoid that is not capable of being naturally produced by a cannabis plant no longer counts as hemp. The clearest example is THC-O, made in a lab from other cannabinoids. In 2023 the DEA said in a letter that it treats THC-O as a controlled substance, not hemp.

Congress told the FDA to publish a list of which cannabinoids the plant makes naturally. It was due Feb 10; the Congressional Research Service said on Oct 28 that it still had not appeared, and we found no sign of it this week. With no official list, shops, lawyers and regulators are making their own calls.

The disputed products: HHC, THCP and delta-8

HHC. In May the DEA listed HHC by name as a Schedule I drug, saying it already counts as a form of THC under federal drug law. Hemp companies are challenging that in two federal appeals courts, but the agency's position is clear, which makes HHC the disputed product with the clearest federal risk.

THCP. Scientists first identified it in a cannabis plant in 2019, so it can occur naturally in tiny amounts, which is why some sellers say it is still hemp. The "33 times stronger than THC" claim comes from a lab test of receptor binding, not from effects in people, and THCP sold today is typically lab-made.

Delta-8. It occurs naturally in small amounts, so under most readings it was not covered on Nov 12. The catch: delta-8 on shelves is typically made by converting CBD in a lab. On Dec 11, cannabinoids made outside the plant stop counting as hemp, and delta-8 also counts toward the new THC cap. It gets hit twice that day.

Is THCA banned now?

Under federal law, no. Today the text defines hemp by delta-9 THC alone, at no more than 0.3 percent by dry weight; THCA is not counted. On Dec 11, hemp is measured by total THC: delta-9 plus about 88 percent of the THCA content under the USDA formula. Flower testing at 20 percent THCA comes out near 17.5 percent total, far above 0.3, so most THCA flower stops being hemp that day. High-CBD flower can fail the same test. The videos calling THCA banned right now are early. Some states already restrict THCA flower on their own, so check your state's law too.

Gummies, drinks and tinctures on Dec 11

Any final product with more than 0.4 mg of total THC plus similar cannabinoids per container stops being hemp. The container is the innermost package touching the product, so the limit is per container, not per serving. A typical gummy or drink has 5 or 10 mg, over 12 times the limit in a single piece. Some full-spectrum CBD products could fail if the whole bottle adds up to more than 0.4 mg of THC; pure CBD isolate with no THC is different. Spectrum News reports no grace period for leftover inventory. Industrial hemp such as fiber and grain stays in the definition: this is about intoxicating products. In Texas, Houston Public Media reports drinkable hemp becomes illegal to sell Dec 11.

The list as of today, and states where cannabis is legal

  • Out since Nov 12: THC-O and other lab-made cannabinoids the plant can't make.
  • Disputed: HHC (DEA says Schedule I) and THCP.
  • Legal federally until Dec 11: THCA flower, delta-8, delta-9 gummies and drinks, and full-spectrum CBD.

State-licensed marijuana sold in licensed dispensaries was never federal hemp, so these rules do not change it. What changes is the hemp aisle: gas stations, convenience stores, online shops and any hemp products on dispensary shelves.

Can Congress still change it?

Dec 11 is also when government funding runs out; the same September stopgap set both dates. Rep. James Comer (R-KY) told Spectrum News a standalone bill is unlikely to get a vote before then and that the best route is a must-pass funding bill. Bills on the table include one from Rep. Beth Van Duyne (R-TX) and Rep. Greg Landsman (D-OH) for hemp drinks and the HEMP Act from Rep. Morgan Griffith (R-VA) and Rep. Marc Veasey (D-TX). Ban backers say products are marketed to kids and shops sell untested products; most of these bills answer with age limits, testing and packaging. Frequently asked questions

Is THCA banned right now?

Not under federal law. THCA is not counted in the current hemp definition. From Dec 11, hemp is measured by total THC, and most THCA flower will exceed the limit.

What was banned on Nov 12?

Cannabinoids that a cannabis plant cannot naturally produce, such as lab-made THC-O, lost federal hemp status.

Is HHC illegal?

The DEA listed HHC as a Schedule I drug in May 2026 and says it counts as a form of THC. Hemp companies are challenging the rule in court.

Does the hemp change affect licensed dispensaries?

No. State-licensed marijuana was never federal hemp, so these hemp rules do not change those programs.
