EPISODE 35 · TUE NOV 17, 2026

Joe Rogan blames Texas's THC ban for the election. Can Congress still stop it?

Rogan says the Texas THC fight was "part of what cost them the election." That is his opinion; here is what he said, what the results show, and why Dec 11 is both the hemp ban date and the day federal funding runs out.

Same day: Dec 11 is when the federal cap of 0.4 mg of total THC per container takes hold for most hemp products, and when the short-term funding that keeps the government open runs out. Rep. James Comer (R-KY) says that must-pass bill is the best shot at more time.

What Rogan said, and what the results show

On JRE MMA Show #188 (posted Nov 11), guest Paddy Pimblett opened a Willie's Remedy THC drink. Rogan called it "legit," said Texas is backwards on cannabis, and called the ban "part of what cost them the election." That is his opinion.

The results: Texas Democrats won the governor's race (Gina Hinojosa, projected, results preliminary until certified), lieutenant governor (Vikki Goodwin), every other statewide office on the ballot and a Texas House majority. Goodwin campaigned on cannabis and criticized Dan Patrick for pushing the THC ban, which Gov. Abbott vetoed. But an election result carries no single reason. Whether THC moved the needle is a fair question, not a settled fact.

Can Congress still stop it?

Rep. James Comer (R-KY) told Spectrum News on Nov 13 he does not expect a standalone hemp bill to get a vote before Dec 11: "The best opportunity, in my opinion, is to get this on a bill that is going to pass." He means another short-term funding bill. The ban came in with last November's funding deal, and in September the stopgap moved most of it from Nov 12 to Dec 11, the day that funding runs out. Hemp language could ride along in a December stopgap, or be left out. Nothing is promised.

Comer is circulating a draft, not a filed bill. As reported by Marijuana Moment, it would delay the ban and add rules: no sales under 21, lab testing, child-resistant packaging, a QR code to test results, and U.S.-grown hemp.

What changed Nov 12, and what changes Dec 11

The 2018 Farm Bill defined hemp as cannabis with no more than 0.3 percent delta-9 THC by dry weight, which opened the door to delta-8, THCA flower and THC drinks. On Nov 12, cannabinoids a cannabis plant cannot make on its own, like THC-O, lost hemp status. Whether HHC is covered is disputed.

On Dec 11, hemp is measured by total THC, which counts THCA (delta-9 plus about 88 percent of THCA under the USDA formula), and any final product over 0.4 mg of total THC and similar cannabinoids per container stops being hemp. The container is the innermost package touching the product, so a ten-pack of 5 mg gummies holds 50 mg, 125 times the limit, and wrapping each gummy separately still leaves one 5 mg gummy over 12 times the limit. Spectrum reports no grace period for leftover inventory.

The bills on the table

None has passed. Rep. Beth Van Duyne (R-TX) and Rep. Greg Landsman (D-OH) would keep hemp-derived THC drinks legal with age limits, testing and a federal tax per milligram. Rep. Morgan Griffith (R-VA) and Rep. Marc Veasey (D-TX) have the HEMP Act, with FDA rules. Rep. Andy Barr (R-KY) and Rep. Angie Craig (D-MN) have another. In the Senate, Sen. Tim Sheehy (R) and Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D) are expected to file a regulation bill. Ban backers say products are marketed to kids and shops sell untested products, and the ban passed in a funding package with votes from both parties. Most bills on the table agree on age limits, testing and packaging; the fight is regulate or ban.

What it means in Texas, and the one call to make

Houston Public Media reports drinkable hemp becomes illegal to sell in Texas on Dec 11 under the federal ban. The Legislature cannot overrule federal law but can shape what follows when the session opens January 12. Goodwin has set aside the first 30 Senate bill numbers for other senators' priorities, with THC regulation on her list, per KSAT. Because this ban is federal, call your U.S. representative and both senators; for Texas lawmakers use our Contact Lawmakers page.

Frequently asked questions

What did Joe Rogan say about Texas and THC?

He said Texas is backwards on cannabis and that the THC ban was "part of what cost them the election." That is his opinion; election results do not come with a single reason.

Why is Dec 11 important for hemp?

It is the date the 0.4 mg total THC per container cap takes hold for most hemp products, and the day short-term federal funding runs out.

Is the 0.4 mg limit per serving?

No. It is per container, the innermost package touching the product.

Can Congress still change the hemp ban before Dec 11?

Possibly, by attaching language to a must-pass funding bill, as Rep. James Comer (R-KY) suggests. Nothing has passed.
