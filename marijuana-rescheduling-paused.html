A Judge Just PAUSED Marijuana Rescheduling — Tuesday, Oct 13 at 6:00 AM Central
EPISODE 2 · OCTOBER 13, 2026

A judge paused marijuana rescheduling. Here's what happens now

Medical marijuana already moved to Schedule III. The rest is stuck at a DEA hearing that just hit pause, while Massachusetts votes on undoing legalization and the federal hemp ban closes in.

Medical moved. Everything else didn't.

In April 2026 the federal government moved state-licensed medical marijuana to Schedule III. Recreational marijuana, and everything outside state medical programs, is still Schedule I.

Moving the rest requires a formal hearing at the DEA. On September 29, the DEA's chief administrative law judge paused it. Opponents of rescheduling asked the judge to consider a new report from the Government Accountability Office, the federal watchdog, that questions how the DEA and FDA handle drug scheduling. The government's response was due October 13. If the judge allows more rounds of arguments, legal experts say the delay could run well past October.

Why it matters

Full Schedule III would end 280E, the federal tax rule that stops cannabis businesses from deducting normal expenses, and would make research much easier.

It would not legalize recreational marijuana. Your state's law still decides what's legal where you live.

Massachusetts Question 8: undoing legalization

Massachusetts voters legalized recreational marijuana in 2016. On November 3, Question 8 asks them to end legal recreational sales. No state has ever shut down a legal recreational market.

  • Polls: a University of New Hampshire poll found 71% plan to vote no and 24% yes. A MassINC poll of likely voters found 61% no, 27% yes and 11% undecided.
  • Who's against it: Governor Maura Healey. The repeal campaign has still out-raised the no side about two to one.
  • If it passes: the retail ban wouldn't start until January 1, 2028, and existing stores could try to convert to medical.

The federal hemp ban countdown

November 12: cannabinoids the plant can't make naturally, like THC-O, stop counting as hemp nationwide. That part was never delayed. December 11: a cap of 0.4 mg of total THC per container, which hits gummies, drinks and THCA flower.

December 11 is also when federal funding runs out, so the next spending bill is the industry's best shot at a fix. A bipartisan coalition of state attorneys general wants the ban kept as written, and Senator Ted Budd of North Carolina says it protects kids. Senator Amy Klobuchar of Minnesota argues an immediate ban would crush farmers and small businesses; the industry wants rules instead, such as 21-and-up sales and potency limits. The FDA was told to publish official cannabinoid lists by February, and industry reports say they still aren't out.

Around the states

  • Maryland: legal sales have reached $917 million this year, up 5%.
  • Minnesota: few farmers are getting into cannabis growing two years in.
  • Oklahoma: regulators retracted an email about flavoring ingredients; no rule changes.
  • Illinois: a bill for direct payments to people harmed by prohibition gained five cosponsors.
  • Rhode Island: hemp growers still don't know what they can sell next year.

What it means for you

  • Medical patients in state programs: nothing changes at the counter.
  • Recreational users: federal law hasn't moved. Your state's law is what matters.
  • Hemp gummies, drinks or THCA flower: mark December 11.

Update (Oct 13, 2026): the episode says delta-8 loses federal hemp status on November 12. After Congress's September 2026 stopgap, only cannabinoids the plant can't make naturally (like THC-O) keep the Nov 12 date. Legal analyses now place delta-8 made from CBD with the December 11 changes, pending an FDA list that still isn't out. This article reflects the update.
