Can Texas legalize marijuana? HB 362, HB 369 and SB 237, explained

Three bills filed in Austin on Monday, November 9, would make marijuana legal under Texas law for adults 21 and older, if they passed. Up to two ounces, twelve plants at home and a 20% tax. Here's what the text actually says, and the path it has to take.

Filed is not law. Marijuana is illegal in Texas today, and two ounces or less is still a crime. Rep. John Bucy filed a nearly identical bill in 2025 (HB 2975); it was sent to committee in March 2025 and never came out.

The three bills

  • HB 362, Rep. Bucy, and HB 369, Rep. Moody, in the House. Both are titled the "Texas Regulation of Cannabis Act." We compared them word by word and found no difference in substance.
  • SB 237, Sen. Alvarado, in the Senate. Similar, with some key differences (below).

What the House bills would allow

For adults 21 and older, the House text would allow:

  • Having up to 2 ounces, with no more than 15 grams as concentrate.
  • Giving up to 2 ounces to another adult for free, as long as it isn't advertised.
  • Growing up to 12 plants at a private home, only in an enclosed area with locks, not visible from a public place.

The text says that conduct, by itself, could not bring arrest, prosecution or a penalty.

The date that surprised us

September 1, 2027: the bills take effect, if passed as written.

July 1, 2028: deadline for the state's rules.

November 1, 2028: deadline for the state to begin licensing growers, shops, transporters and testing labs.

Those 2028 dates are outer deadlines, not start dates. But at the outer limit, that leaves a gap of up to fourteen months when adults could have it and grow it, with no licensed place to buy.

Taxes, shops and local control

The House bills add a 20% tax on retail sales, on top of regular sales tax, plus a separate 15% when a grower sells to a shop. The money would go:

  • 25% to the veterans' assistance fund
  • 15% to chemical dependency prevention and treatment
  • 10% to a local share account
  • The rest to the permanent school fund

No one under 21 could enter or work at a shop, and selling or giving cannabis to anyone under 21 would be a misdemeanor. Cities and counties could not ban licensed businesses or unreasonably restrict them, but could set hours, locations and how many.

What the House text would not authorize

  • Driving while intoxicated.
  • Consuming cannabis in a car on a public road, an aircraft in flight or a boat on a public waterway.
  • Consuming it in a public place, unless a city or county designates an area closed to anyone under 21, or anywhere people under 21 can get in.
  • Possessing or consuming it at schools, child-care centers or correctional facilities.

Property owners and managers could still prohibit it on their property.

What the bills leave out

  • Hemp: hemp and hemp products are excluded from the bills' definition of cannabis. These bills don't touch hemp shops.
  • Past records: we searched all three texts and found nothing to clear past records.
  • Federal law: state bills can't change it.

How SB 237 is different

  • Allows 2.5 ounces instead of 2 (still 15 grams of concentrate and 12 plants, 21+).
  • No tax section.
  • Requires a county election before growers, shops or labs can operate in that county. The House bills do the opposite and stop local bans.
  • Selling to someone under 21 is a Class C misdemeanor, a lighter charge than the House bills.
  • Same effective date and licensing deadlines.

Can it pass? The path it has to take

A House bill goes to a House committee, then the full House, a Senate committee, the full Senate, and then the governor. A Senate bill runs the same path in the other order. All of it has to happen between January 12, 2027, when the session opens, and May 31, 2027, when it ends. Bills aren't sent to committee until the session opens, so there will be no committee vote this year.

The first committee is where Bucy's 2025 bill stopped. A committee chair decides which bills get a hearing. In the Senate, Republicans held their majority, 19 to 12 in the AP count we saw. In the House, early counts had Democrats leading; we haven't seen a final count. We're not predicting the outcome. We'll follow every step on our Texas bill tracker.

How to weigh in

  • Find your state representative and senator with the Who Represents Me tool.
  • A short, respectful call or email works: say you're a constituent, name the bill number, say whether you support or oppose it, and give one reason in your own words.
  • If a bill gets a committee hearing, the notice is posted on Texas Legislature Online. The House has a witness registration site where you can set up a profile ahead of time.

Also filed Monday

  • HB 507 (Bucy): bars state agencies, cities and counties from requiring cannabis drug tests or asking about medical low-THC or hemp use, with exceptions for peace officers and workers under federal transportation testing rules. Private employers aren't covered.
  • HB 330 (Plesa): adds a condition that causes menstrual cramps to the Texas medical (low-THC) program's list.
  • HB 667 (Moody): penalties for marijuana concentrate. We haven't covered it yet.

Frequently asked questions

Is marijuana legal in Texas now?

No. These are filed bills, not law. Marijuana is illegal in Texas today.

Is delta-8 legal in Texas?

These bills don't cover hemp products. See our delta-8 in Texas guide and the federal hemp ban explainer.

Can I grow marijuana in Texas?

Not today. HB 362, HB 369 and SB 237 would allow up to 12 plants at home for adults 21+ only if they became law, starting no earlier than September 1, 2027.
