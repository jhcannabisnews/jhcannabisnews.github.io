EPISODE 42 · TUE NOV 24, 2026

The hemp ban hits THC drinks Dec 11. Will Congress save them?

On Dec 11 a 5 mg THC seltzer becomes 12.5 times over the federal limit, and the law names the can. Here is what the law covers, who is pushing each way, and what no agency has answered about drinks people already own.

The math: divide the milligrams by 0.4. A 5 mg drink is 12.5 times over the limit, a 10 mg can 25 times, a 100 mg can 250 times. Each can is judged on its own. Government funding runs out the same day, Dec 11.

What the law says, can by can

The 0.4 mg cap came in last November's funding law; the Dec 11 date came from a second funding law in September. A final hemp product is not hemp if it has more than 0.4 mg of total THC per container, and total includes THCA. The law defines container as the package touching the product: a jar, a bottle, a box, and a can. So every can is judged on its own; a 12-pack does not spread it out. The law also reaches drink mixes, powders and liquids meant to be mixed in and sold directly to shoppers.

Spencer Fane, a law firm advising Texas businesses, wrote last month that unless Congress acts again, most THC drinks now sold in Texas venues will be federally unlawful on Dec 11. What is left: drinks at or under 0.4 mg per can, CBD drinks with no real THC, and drinks sold in state-licensed marijuana stores, which were never federal hemp. Some brands have set end dates; VICE reported Five is ending its seltzer line on Dec 11.

Who is fighting to keep Dec 11

Sen. James Lankford (R-OK) sent colleagues a letter and a Nov 20 release saying "A vote to extend the THC limits is a vote for nationwide legalization of marijuana" and "It is critical that this ban goes into effect on December eleventh." His release cites the Journal of Medical Toxicology: half of the delta-8 exposures it counted in 2024 were children, the safety case ban backers make. Sens. Ted Budd (R-NC) and Pete Ricketts (R-NE) say they won't support any funding package that delays the limits or builds a new framework. Trade press also reported groups from the beverage trade urging no extension; we have not seen that letter ourselves.

Who is fighting to save the drinks

A national retailer is running an in-store and billboard campaign urging shoppers to contact Congress; its government relations executive told WRAL the worry is "the short number of days left on the congressional calendar." Willie Nelson, whose company sells a hemp THC drink, told High Times, "Banning hemp on December eleventh won't solve problems, smart regulation will."

Two bills to know, neither moved. One, from Rep. Beth Van Duyne (R-TX) and Rep. Greg Landsman (D-OH), is written for drinks: 21 and over, 5 mg per serving, federal beverage tax and distribution rules, and compliant drinks exempt from the 0.4 mg limit. The other, from Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-MT) and Amy Klobuchar (D-MN), would replace the limit with new rules, but it does not delay Dec 11 and has had no hearing. See our Hemp Guardrails Act explainer.

Can Congress save them? Eight House days

The path lawmakers keep pointing to is the next stopgap funding bill. Rep. James Comer (R-KY) has said the best chance is to attach hemp language to a must-pass funding bill. As of Monday night we had not seen funding text with hemp in it. The House is not in session this week and its calendar shows eight scheduled days between now and Dec 11, counting the 11th. So not on its own; the realistic route is that bill, and only if leaders include hemp. Watch whether the stopgap mentions hemp, whether Lankford, Budd or Ricketts withhold support from a bill that delays, and whether the DEA, FDA or Justice Department says anything about products already owned.

Drinks people already own

The law has no grace period and no buy-back, and says nothing about products bought before Dec 11. Its text treats cannabis that is not hemp as marijuana, and federal law makes possessing marijuana a crime on paper; whether that applies to a can bought while it was legal is exactly what no agency has said. We found no guidance from the DEA, FDA or Justice Department as of Monday night. Selling and shipping are clearer: after Dec 11 an over-the-limit drink is not hemp. Wild Revival, a Denton THC drink company, told local reporters its payment processor dropped it over the coming ban. What to do is your call and a lawyer's, not ours.

Where Texas stands

Texas allows hemp THC drinks for adults 21 and over; stores must register with the state health department and check ID. But Texas's 0.3 percent hemp rule is still on the books while federal law counts milligrams per can, and we found no ruling on whether a Texas shop can keep selling a drink over the federal limit. Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa (D) told the Texas Tribune she would revoke the under-21 hemp THC ban Gov. Abbott put in place. Dates: Dec 11 (federal change and funding deadline), Jan 12 (Legislature), Jan 19 (new governor). Regulate, Don't Ban: a per-can limit, testing, honest labels and an ID check make more sense than a drink that is legal on the 10th and not the 11th.

Frequently asked questions

Are THC drinks illegal on Dec 11?

Under federal law, a hemp product with more than 0.4 mg of total THC per container is not hemp, and the law names cans. A 5 mg drink is 12.5 times over.

Is the limit per can or per pack?

Per container, meaning each can on its own. A 12-pack does not spread it out.

Can Congress still save THC drinks?

Possibly through the next stopgap funding bill, but we had seen no funding text with hemp language, and the House has 8 scheduled days before Dec 11.

What happens to drinks I already own?

The law says nothing about products bought before Dec 11, and we found no agency guidance. Ask a lawyer.
