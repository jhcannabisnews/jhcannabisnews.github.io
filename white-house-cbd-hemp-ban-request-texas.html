EPISODE 43 · WED NOV 25, 2026

Will the White House save CBD from the hemp ban, but not THC?

On Monday night the White House sent Congress its requests for the next short-term funding bill. As reported, one line asks to allow "nonintoxicating full-spectrum CBD products with guardrails." It names no THC gummies, drinks or THCA flower. What it means in Texas.

We haven't seen the document. This is as reported by The Hill and Marijuana Moment, which says it is not clear exactly which products it would cover. As reported, it does not name THC gummies, drinks or THCA flower, which still hit the 0.4 mg per-container limit on Dec 11.

What the request says, as reported

The request is a list of changes for the next short-term funding bill, from missile defense money to one line on hemp. According to The Hill and Marijuana Moment, that line asks Congress to update the definition of a finished hemp product to allow "nonintoxicating full spectrum CBD products with guardrails," while still restricting products that "pose serious health risks." Full-spectrum CBD products, often oils, keep the plant's other compounds, including a little THC. Under the law starting Dec 11, a whole container cannot have more than 0.4 mg of THC, so even some CBD oils fail. That is the gap the request appears aimed at. Marijuana Moment notes it is not clear which products it would cover.

What stays the same in Texas, and the gray zone

Texas writes its own hemp definition into state law: 0.3 percent delta-9 THC by dry weight, which Washington's Dec 11 change does not rewrite. The state health department's rules counting THCA took effect March 31 and have been tied up in court, with enforcement unclear. But federal law still applies in Texas: from Dec 11, a product over the federal limit is not hemp under federal law even if it passes Texas's test, and no ruling says whether a Texas shop can keep selling it.

On Dec 12, as we read it, what is clearly allowed under both: products at or under 0.4 mg of THC per container, like many CBD products, plus hemp seed and fiber. Texas's Compassionate Use Program is a separate state program for patients, under its own rules. Everything in between is the gray zone. News, not legal advice.

Where Texas's senators stand, and the fight over the request

On Nov 20, 14 Republican senators wrote to President Trump: no delay and no new framework, saying "On December eleventh, this dangerous experiment must end." Sen. John Cornyn (R-TX) signed; Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX) did not. As Marijuana Moment reported, Cruz voted last year to keep hemp THC products legal and said regulation "should rest with each individual state."

Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR) posted that the request "proposes to keep gas station candy marijuana on the shelves" and warned another delay, which he says the administration promised in August not to seek, would "threaten the entire bill." The reported wording does not say where its line sits, since full-spectrum CBD still carries a trace of THC; Cotton reads that as a loophole, and we cannot settle it without the document. Sens. Ted Budd (R-NC) and Pete Ricketts (R-NE) already said they won't support a package that delays the limits. Ban backers like Budd say these products are reaching kids. Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) said of the funding talks, "No deals yet," and the House calendar shows eight scheduled days before Dec 11.

How this could go

  1. Congress passes a funding bill with the White House's CBD change: some CBD oils survive and THC products still hit the limit on Dec 11.
  2. The funding bill says nothing about hemp: the full limit lands Dec 11, CBD oils included.
  3. Someone pushes a wider delay that covers THC, which Cotton, Budd and Ricketts say they will fight; we haven't seen it in writing.

As of Tuesday night, no funding bill text we have seen mentions hemp. Dec 11 is also the day government funding runs out. Regulate, Don't Ban: a 21 age limit, lab testing and honest labels make more sense than a product that is legal Dec 10 and not Dec 11.

Texas HB 1224: a 2027 defense

Rep. Nicole Collier (D-Fort Worth) filed House Bill 1224 on Nov 18. Someone would not be guilty of a possession offense if the label says legal consumable hemp, the product turns out to contain marijuana or a controlled substance over the hemp limits, and it was bought from a store the buyer reasonably believed could sell hemp. It takes effect Sept 1, 2027, so it protects nobody on Dec 11, and it is a defense raised after a charge, not a shield from arrest; a judge or jury decides whether the belief was reasonable.

Why it matters: in Texas, two ounces or less of marijuana is a Class B misdemeanor (up to 180 days in jail and a $2,000 fine), and THC concentrate is treated more harshly: under a gram is a state jail felony, and one to four grams a third-degree felony, weighed with whatever the THC is mixed with. This is Collier's fourth try. The Senate remains 19 to 12 Republican; Lt. Gov.-elect Vikki Goodwin (D) said, "I'm not interested in a full ban on THC." If you face a charge, talk to a Texas lawyer.

One more date: the DEA's Nov 29 deadline

Sunday, Nov 29 is a DEA deadline for state-licensed medical marijuana companies. Companies in Utah, Arizona, Oklahoma and Pennsylvania asked a federal appeals court to pause it, and the Justice Department filed opposition Monday; as of Tuesday we found no ruling. No source we found says it changes anything for Texas patients yet. Dates to circle: Nov 29, Dec 11, and Jan 12, when the Texas Legislature meets.
