Will Texas legalize marijuana in 2027? What the math says

Eighteen. Under Texas Senate rules today, that is how many of 31 senators it takes to move a bill to the front of the line. Republicans will hold 19 seats and Democrats 12. Three adult-use marijuana bills are filed, and the Legislature has 140 days from January 12. Here is the math, with no odds and no predictions.

The number that decides the Senate

Senate Rule 5.13 sets the vote to suspend the regular order of business at five-ninths of the members present: 18 when all 31 are in the room. The Senate has changed that number before. In 2015 it fell from two-thirds of the chamber to three-fifths, and in 2021, by an 18-13 vote, to five-ninths, KSAT reported. If all 12 Democrats voted yes, a bill would still need six Republicans. The 2027 rules have not been adopted, so the number could change.

On Monday, Nov 9, outgoing Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick backed Republican senators changing the Senate's rulebook, telling Texas Public Radio: "The lieutenant governor derives her power from the Senate." He said 16 of 31 senators, a simple majority, can change the rules. He did not say which powers. The Texas Tribune reported that those powers include picking committee chairs and deciding which bills reach the floor. We found no rules change adopted yet.

What Goodwin said

Lt. Gov.-elect Vikki Goodwin, who is sworn in January 19, a week after the session opens, was asked by KSAT about legalizing hemp, THC and marijuana. She said: "I am open to it." She also said that making it illegal means "you push people to the black market," that "safety is my primary concern," and that eight years in the House minority taught her to "negotiate." The first 30 Senate bill numbers are reserved for her priorities, and regulating THC is on the list. She did not name a marijuana bill.

Open to it is not a vote.

The three roads

  1. A bill. A House majority (76 of 150), a path through the Senate (18 to bring it up), then the governor. In the House, the counts we saw have Democrats leading 76 to 61, with about a dozen races still uncalled.
  2. A constitutional amendment. Article XVII requires "a vote of two-thirds of all the members elected to each House," then a majority of the votes cast. That works out to 100 House members and 21 senators. If every Democrat voted yes, it would take about two dozen Republicans in the House and nine in the Senate. Marijuana Moment reported that Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa wants voters to decide. We found no statement from her on signing a legalization bill.
  3. The veto. The governor has 10 days (Sundays excepted). An override takes two-thirds of the members present in the house of origin and two-thirds of the members of the other house.

The other side

Sen. Charles Perry, who helped lead the 2025 ban push, has said he plans to try again, First Alert 7 reported. Ban backers say it is about children. In a Dec 4, 2024 statement, Patrick said stores "targeted Texas children and exposed them to dangerous levels of THC." Agriculture Commissioner-elect Clayton Tucker told the Texas Standard, "Hemp, I think, is going to be our saving grace," and cited drought tolerance. He did not mention THC.

How New Mexico did it

New Mexico went through its legislature, not a ballot. Its medical law (SB 523) passed in 2007. In 2019, HB 356 passed the House 36-34 on March 7, a Senate committee sent it forward two days later, and the legislature's records show no Senate Finance report and no floor vote. It died. In 2021, HB 12 passed the House 39-31 on February 26, and two Senate committees sent it forward, the last on March 18. The Senate never voted on it before the regular session ended.

Then the governor called a special session. HB 2 was introduced March 30 and passed both chambers March 31, 2021, 38-32 in the House and 22-15 in the Senate. It was signed April 12. Licensed adult-use sales began April 1, 2022. Texas is not New Mexico, and we are not predicting a copy, but that path ran through a legislature.

What Texans told pollsters

A University of Houston Hobby School survey of 1,200 Texas adults in January 2025 found 62% support for legislation to legalize the sale and use of marijuana for recreational purposes, and 55% support for a proposed ban on the currently unregulated sale of THC consumables. It also found 69% for decriminalizing possession by adults 21 and older, and 79% for legalizing marijuana for medical purposes with a doctor's prescription. The margin of error is about 2.8 points. Question wording matters, and the poll is nearly two years old. We found no newer statewide poll asking it.

Hemp ban bills filed: none that we found

As of our 5:15 AM check on Saturday, Nov 14, we found no bill to ban hemp products and no bill to regulate them on their own. Filing runs to March 12, 2027, and the session ends around May 31. If that changes, we will say so.

So will Texas legalize marijuana in 2027? Marijuana is still illegal in Texas today. Anyone who hands you a percentage is guessing. The first real test is a Senate rules vote in January.
